## Affix pairings

Affixes come in **mirrored pairs** — reverse the letters, invert the meaning.

| Prefix | Meaning            | Suffix | Meaning            |
| ------ | ------------------ | ------ | ------------------ |
| AJ     | first              | JA     | last               |
| MA     | small              | AM     | large              |
| OV     | toward             | VO     | away               |
| NEV    | new / the start of | VEN    | old / the end of   |
| EL     | I / me\*           | LE     | a lack of myself\* |
| UL     | Will               | LU     | Won't              |

\* When paired with a verb can mean **I will** or **I won't** — similar to **UL / LU**.

Early Erajan had no verbs, so verbs are nearly all English loan-words, used for
physical actions. Prefixes modify what follows, suffixes what precedes, and they
stack (`Treivenven` = tree-end-old = "the branches of the tree are old").

### Not on the board — inferred from the letter

| Affix       | Meaning                                                            | Evidence                                                                 |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **OR / RO** | to, for / from                                                      | `Or-` ×5 word-initial, `-ro` ×2 word-final in [[Erajan Letter]]           |
| **D**       | the copula / genitive — "be", "of". `ELD` = my, `ULD` = will be     | a single letter is its own mirror, so it must be a neutral, uninvertible word |
| **ORO**     | always / both ways (?)                                              | palindrome of OR/RO — same logic as **D**. Only in `Uldeinjororo`         |

## Worked examples

From the Foreign Language Class poster:

- **ajlor** = AJ (first) + LOR (word) → "first word", i.e. hello
- **nevdau** = NEV (start of) + DAU (day) → "dawn"
- **Ulgo?** = UL (will) + *go* (loan-word verb) → "will go?"

## The letter

Full artefact: [[Erajan Letter]] — found at Clara Epsen's grave in the [[Tomb]].

```
Oreldajiris,
Ulelddau, uleldnai, uleldmora.
Orulelgift eldhew oriss. Nevdau uldareile.
Nevdau eldareilu. Ajelbid ulheed eldlor: Uloddiris.
Hewsinclairlu. Hewepsenlu. . . Forget uldein inn.
Uldjorjeari.. Uldarei orissfennro. Uldeinjororo.
Oreraja. Oriss nevarei.

                    Lorja eldiris,
                              Uldjora
```

> [!note] Transcription fix
> Previously read as `Nerdau` / `nerarei`. That glyph is a **v**, not an r — it
> matches the pointed stroke in the `w` of `hew`, not the flat shoulder of the
> `r` in `Oriss` or `Forget`. So **`Nevdau`** (= dawn, per the poster) and
> **`nevarei`**. Those two words were what blocked the hardest lines.

### Plain English

> To my first love,
> I will be day, I will be night, I will be tomorrow.
> I will gift you my house. Dawn will be a future without me; dawn, and I have no future.
> First, I bid you heed my words: *…my love.*
> The house won't be Sinclair's. The house won't be Epsen's… Forget…
> …The future will be yours, from Fenn. It will be yours alone, always.
> To Eraja. To you, a new future.
>
> Last words, my love,
> Ever yours

A farewell note **and** a will: the writer is going at dawn, the house passes to
the recipient, and explicitly **not** to House Sinclair or House Epsen. "To Eraja"
does double duty — the [[House of Orinda|history]] calls Eraja the southern
"mystical realm of the ancestors", so it also means joining the dead.

### Uldjora is not a name

`UL·D·JOR·A` is built exactly like `LOR·JA` ("last word") on the line above it,
and **JOR** recurs in two other sentences. It parses as a sign-off — "[I] will be
yours" — not a signature. There is no Uldjora to identify.

### Names in the letter

`oriss`, `fenn` and `eraja` all echo the [[House of Orinda]] list, but in context
they parse as grammar, not people — `OR·ISS` = "to you", `OR·ERA·JA` = "to the
last era". The overlap with **Oris** (first king, and the township named for him)
may be deliberate wordplay by whoever wrote the letter.
